Common questions

15 questions

Why do I owe money on a claim?

Until you meet your deductible ($1,500 a person this year), you pay the plan's price for most care. After that you pay a copay or a share of the cost (coinsurance) until you reach the out-of-pocket maximum.

Each claim's details show what the provider billed, what the plan paid, and why you owe what you do.

See your claims

What's an EOB, and is it a bill?

An Explanation of Benefits (EOB) shows how we processed a claim: what was billed, what we paid and what you may owe. It isn't a bill. Your provider sends the bill, and it should match the "You owe" amount on the EOB.

See processed claims

A claim needs more information. What do I do?

We'll tell you exactly what's missing, often a form like the accident form. Upload it from the claim, or reply to our message with a photo of it. Once we have it, we finish the claim within 5 business days.

Open the claim that needs a form

How long does a claim take?

Most claims are done within 30 days of when your provider sends them, many much sooner. You can follow each step (received, in review, processed) on the claim.

See your claims

What's the difference between my deductible and out-of-pocket max?

Your deductible ($1,500 a person) is what you pay before the plan pays more for most care. Your out-of-pocket maximum ($4,000 a person) is the most you'll pay in a year for covered, in-network care. After that, covered care is $0.

See where you stand

Do I need a referral to see a specialist?

No. Your PPO plan doesn't need referrals. An in-network specialist visit is a $50 copay. Some tests and procedures need approval first (a prior authorization); your doctor's office asks for it.

See what you pay

What is a prior authorization?

Some care, like an MRI or a planned surgery, needs our approval before you get it. Your doctor sends the request, a nurse reviewer looks at it, and you both hear back, usually within 14 days.

See your approvals

I lost my ID card. What do I do?

Your digital ID card works everywhere a plastic one does, so you can keep getting care. To get a new plastic card, choose Order a card under the card. It arrives in 7 to 10 days.

Go to ID cards

Does everyone on my plan get their own card?

Yes. Everyone on your plan has their own medical, dental and vision card with their own member ID. Use Viewing on the ID cards page to see each person's cards.

Go to ID cards

Which card do I use at the pharmacy?

Use your medical card. The Rx BIN, PCN and group on the front tell the pharmacy how to bill your plan.

Show your medical card

Can I get a 90-day supply of my medicine?

Yes, for medicines you take every day. Ask your doctor for a 90-day prescription, then fill it at the pharmacy or have it mailed to you with the mail order form.

Get the mail order form

My doctor's office is closed. Where should I go?

For colds, rashes and refills, a video visit is $0 and open 24/7. For sprains, cuts and fevers, urgent care is $50. For chest pain, trouble breathing or severe injuries, go to the emergency room ($250) or call 911.

Compare your options

How do I know if a doctor is in network?

Search for them on Find care. Every result there is in network for your plan. Care outside the network costs more, so check before you book.

Find a doctor

How do I change my address?

Go to Addresses in My Account. New ID cards and mailed letters go to the address there, so update it before you order a card.

Update your address

Who can see my claims and messages?

Only you, and the Tornado Health team helping you. Adults on your plan sign in with their own account. You can see your children's information until they turn 18.
